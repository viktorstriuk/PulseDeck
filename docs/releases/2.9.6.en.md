# PulseDeck 2.9.6

## Library and Windows

- Folder relocation opens the destination with write access before `fsync`. Permission errors are not swallowed; transaction journals and rollback remain intact.
- Folder and playlist navigation patches existing buttons and SVG nodes instead of rebuilding entire rails. Node identity, focus and handlers are retained.
- Setup brands the staged, verified executable's version resources with PulseDeck product/file descriptions, original filename and version. App identity and matching shortcuts are consistent. Other applications' shortcuts are untouched; the application does not pin itself. An old Windows pin with a cached name may require unpinning and pinning once.
- Update restart and “Launch PulseDeck” sanitize inherited Node-mode flags and launch a visible application. A one-time acknowledgement from a visible window, not process creation alone, confirms launch. Errors retain retry/report actions. A launch retry does not reinstall an already updated application. The graphical installer waits for its child process's output streams to close before reporting completion.

## Music videos

- Attach a separate music video without replacing the song or artwork. Search YouTube, edit the query or paste a link, then trim before saving. Only managed yt-dlp, FFmpeg and FFprobe are used.
- A bottom-player button and configurable **Alt+Shift+V** shortcut switch between audio and video. The center is a video surface without native video controls. The existing player controls pause, seeking, repeat, volume and output. Escape returns to audio. Both sources retain independent durations.
- Adjust offset and speed, link selected positions, nudge by ±0.10 seconds and compare audio/video. Local automatic alignment compares multiple spectral and waveform regions, estimating offset and small clock drift. Uncertain matches and inconsistent edits are not applied. Different live, studio and re-edited versions are not guaranteed to align.
- Handover prepares the second source, maps its position and ramps the sound. Outside the shared timeline, switching is rejected instead of jumping to an unrelated position. Pause and track changes cancel unfinished handovers.
- The existing YouTube audio trim dialog now offers “Add music video.” Audio and video trims are independent. For the same source, the initial mapping follows the audio trim. A failed video save retains an already downloaded audio track; retry does not duplicate that download.
- Track context menus offer video search. Playlist “More” offers a candidate search after explicit consent. Candidates are not automatically downloaded or assigned without user selection.
- Source limits: one YouTube video, up to 30 minutes and 2 GiB; up to three prepared sources. Saved clips are self-contained VP9/Opus WebM files, up to 1920 pixels wide. Live streams, DRM and bypassing source restrictions are unsupported.

## Lyrics backgrounds

- “Find” and “Find music video” join “Choose your background.” Image search reuses the existing cover picker but writes only the independent lyrics background.
- A clip can also be the lyrics background. It is muted and follows song time and alignment instead of looping independently.
- Clips persist even without lyrics. Protecting a playlist encrypts its video/background assets, deduplicating a shared reference. Lock revokes streaming access. Folder moves and explicit unprotection retain the association.

## Sound

- New **Settings → Sound** page: system-default output, device selection, refresh and sound test. A newly connected output offers a one-click switch. Disconnection falls back to the system output while retaining the preferred device for reconnection. Microphone permission is not requested.
- One audio path for library playback, videos, online previews, audio/video trimming and the sound test. Master volume, mute and output selection apply to all these sources.
- Nondestructive integrated loudness and true-peak normalization using managed FFmpeg. References: first played track in the current playlist, first track in playlist order, fixed level or an explicitly chosen track. Normalization is opt-in and disabled by default.
- Adjustable tolerance and maximum boost. Correction uses a separate gain stage with peak headroom; it does not overwrite manual volume. Effective level and a dB adjustment appear beside the slider. Late analysis cannot seize a dragged volume thumb.
- Track context menu: “Normalize to this track.” The sound test plays a short part of a user recording, or a quiet ramped tone in an empty library. An audio/video pair also matches the video's level to the audio when global normalization is off.
- Analysis has bounded concurrency, cancellation on lock and recording-aware caches. PCM and source files are not uploaded for analysis. Initial uncached analysis can delay playback. Network previews are estimated over the available first 20 seconds, not claimed to be fully measured streams.
